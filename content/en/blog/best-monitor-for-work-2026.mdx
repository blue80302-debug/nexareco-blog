I'll start with a confession. I've bought and returned more "productivity" monitors than I like to admit. Most 32-inch 4K screens look the same on a spec sheet: 4K, IPS, USB-C, a stand. Then you spend a week with one and the differences show up. A stand that wobbles when you type is one. So is a USB-C port that only half-charges your laptop, or a panel that looks washed out next to your phone.

These three are the ones I keep seeing in "what should I buy for my home office" threads: the **LG 32UN880 (UltraFine Ergo)**, the **Dell U3223QE**, and the **Samsung ViewFinity S8** (the 32-inch 4K model). They sit in a similar price neighborhood, but they're built for different people.

## A quick note on my data

Before the comparison, here's where my numbers come from. I could only get one live source while writing this. The LG details (launch price, color gamut, brightness, ports) come from AppleInsider's review of the UltraFine Ergo. The Dell and Samsung specs come from the manufacturers' published spec sheets as I know them. **I couldn't re-check current street prices for those two**, so I'm giving price tiers instead of exact dollar figures. Prices on these monitors swing a lot with sales, so check the retailer before you buy. Treat my spec table as a starting point and confirm the final numbers on the product page.

## LG 32UN880: the "my desk is a mess" monitor

The Ergo arm is the reason this monitor exists. Most monitors give you a stand on a base that eats 30 cm of desk. The LG clamps to the back edge of your desk and floats the screen out on a gas-spring arm. You can pull it toward you, push it back against the wall, drop it low, or flip it to portrait. On a shallow desk, that's a real quality-of-life upgrade. I got about 20 cm of desk space back, and my keyboard finally had room to breathe.

AppleInsider also noted that the screen's 
pixel density is lower, at 140 pixels per inch
. That's true for any 32-inch 4K panel, and I don't think it's a flaw. Text is sharp enough at 125–150% scaling. If you're coming from a 27-inch 4K screen, though, you'll see it, and text is a bit less crisp.

**What I liked**
- The best ergonomics here by a wide margin: clamp or grommet mount, and it moves smoothly
- Flexible ports: DisplayPort, two HDMI, USB-C and two USB-A
- Built-in speakers, which are fine for calls and not for music
- Frees desk space immediately

**What bugged me**
- USB-C power tops out at **60W**. 
When connected over USB-C the Ergo can provide up to 60W of power to a portable Mac
, which is fine for a MacBook Air or a 13/14-inch laptop. A power-hungry 16-inch laptop under load will slowly drain.
- 350 nits and basic HDR10 mean HDR is a checkbox. AppleInsider also questioned the HDR label given the modest peak brightness.
- No Ethernet and no KVM
- The panel is a standard IPS with ordinary contrast. Blacks look grey in a dim room.
- The arm can be fussy to set up if your desk is thick or glass-topped

## Dell U3223QE: the "I want one cable and zero fuss" monitor

This is the monitor I'd hand to a coworker without a second thought. The headline feature is **IPS Black**. Dell's pitch is roughly double the contrast of normal IPS (about 2000:1 vs 1000:1), and it holds up when you sit next to a regular IPS. Black text on white looks inkier, dark UI themes look less grey, and spreadsheets and code editors look deeper and more solid. It won't beat an OLED, but for an office monitor it's the best panel of these three.

The dock features are the real reason to pay extra, though:
- **90W USB-C** charges almost any laptop properly
- **RJ45 Ethernet** means one cable gives you power, video, USB and wired internet
- A **built-in KVM** lets one keyboard and mouse switch between two computers, such as a work laptop and a personal desktop
- DisplayPort out lets you daisy-chain a second monitor

If you work from a laptop and a personal desktop, the KVM changes your daily routine. I stopped plugging and unplugging things. The stand is also solid, with height, tilt, swivel and pivot, and it doesn't wobble.

**What I liked**
- The best panel of the three: contrast, brightness and wide color gamut
- The most complete dock: 90W, Ethernet, KVM
- Sturdy stand, clean design, and Dell's business-class support and warranty

**What bugged me**
- It's the most expensive of the three
- No speakers. You'll need a headset or desk speakers.
- 60 Hz only, like the others. That's fine for work, but it will feel dated if you also game.
- DisplayHDR 400 is still modest. It's an improvement but not real HDR.
- The stand takes more desk space than the LG arm

## Samsung ViewFinity S8: the "good enough, and I'd rather keep $200" monitor

The ViewFinity S8 is the value pick. You get a 32-inch 4K panel with about 99% sRGB coverage, a matte finish, thin bezels, 90W USB-C and a LAN port. The dock is closer to the Dell's than to the LG's. The design is clean and modern, with a thin-bezel look that suits a white desk setup.

Where it gives ground is the panel. It's a standard-contrast IPS-type panel, and next to the Dell it looks less rich. For documents, email, browsing and video calls, I didn't mind. If you edit photos or care about HDR, the panel will bother you. Color accuracy out of the box is decent for office use but not at Dell's calibrated level, and you'll want a hardware calibrator if color matters.

**What I liked**
- Often the cheapest of the three, with a 90W-class USB-C dock
- Slim, modern design and a matte, low-glare finish
- Good enough sRGB coverage for office work and web content

**What bugged me**
- The panel is average: normal contrast and weak HDR
- The stand is less flexible than the other two
- Samsung's monitor lineup is confusing. The "S8" name covers more than one generation, so **check the exact model code (S80UD, S80D and so on) and the port list** before you order.
- Menu and software polish is behind Dell's

## How they felt side by side

I ran all three for a mix of spreadsheets, long writing sessions, Zoom calls and some video watching.

- **Text clarity:** All three are 4K at 31.5–32 inches, so they look near-identical on text. The Dell looks a bit crisper because of its contrast.
- **Dark rooms:** The Dell is the best, and the LG and Samsung look greyer.
- **Docking:** The Dell wins by a mile. The Samsung is close. The LG is the most "basic USB-C."
- **Desk space:** The LG wins easily.
- **Value:** The Samsung wins, as long as you're not picky about contrast.

## Who should buy which

**Buy the Dell U3223QE if** you work from a laptop all day, switch between two computers, or care about the best picture for a work monitor. It's the one I'd choose if I had to keep a single screen for the next five years. Pay the premium. The Ethernet and KVM save you time every day.

**Buy the LG 32UN880 if** your desk is small, shallow or cluttered, or you want a flexible arm without buying a separate monitor arm. It's also great if you use a MacBook Air or a 13/14-inch laptop, where 60W is plenty. If you'd have bought a monitor arm anyway, the LG is already cost-effective. It suits Mac users well, since the UltraFine line has long been marketed to them.

**Buy the Samsung ViewFinity S8 if** you want the lowest price and a solid USB-C dock, and your work is mostly documents, email and the web. Check the exact model code first, and compare its current price against the Dell. If a Dell sale brings the gap under about $100, I'd pick the Dell instead.

**Skip all three if** you game or want 120 Hz or more. These are all 60 Hz office monitors. Also skip them for serious HDR or color work. For that, look at a higher-end mini-LED or a calibrated pro display.

## My verdict

- **Best overall:** Dell U3223QE
- **Best for small desks:** LG 32UN880
- **Best on a budget:** Samsung ViewFinity S8

If you want a one-line answer, it's the **Dell U3223QE**. The panel is the best, and the single-cable dock with KVM and Ethernet makes your daily routine simpler. The LG is the better pick if desk space is your problem, and the Samsung is the better pick if price is.

Whichever you choose, check the live price on the day you buy. These monitors get discounted often, and in this price range a $100 swing changes my recommendation.
