## I Used Both for Six Months. Here's What Happened.

Let me be real: I went into this expecting Obsidian to win. I'd been a Notion power user for three years, and the grass was looking very green on the Obsidian side. Everyone in the PKM (Personal Knowledge Management) community swears by it. So I committed to running both apps in parallel for six months.

The verdict? They're not really competing with each other. That's the honest answer nobody wants to hear. Notion is a team workspace with AI bolted on. Obsidian is a personal knowledge engine with a thriving plugin ecosystem. Comparing them head-to-head is like comparing Slack to a journal — they solve fundamentally different problems.

But since you're here, let's break it down properly.

## The Price Tag

| Feature | Notion | Obsidian |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Free tier | Yes (limited for personal) | Yes (personal use) |
| Paid plan | Plus $10/mo | Sync $50/yr (~$4.2/mo) |
| AI feature | AI add-on $10/mo | Community plugins (free + API costs) |
| Total with AI | ~$20/mo | ~$4.2/mo + $2-5/mo API |
| Team plan | Business $18/user/mo | N/A (personal tool) |
| Publishing | Built-in | Publish $16/mo |

Here's the thing — Notion AI gets expensive fast. The Plus plan is $10/month, and the AI add-on is another $10. That's $20/month just for personal use with AI. Obsidian's Sync is $50/year, and if you want AI, you plug in your own OpenAI or Anthropic API key through community plugins. Most people spend $2-5/month on API calls. The cost difference is real.

## AI Features: Built-in vs. DIY

Notion AI is seamless. It lives right inside your pages — summarize, translate, draft, brainstorm, all without leaving the editor. The killer feature is the Q&A: you can ask questions across your entire workspace and it pulls answers from your actual data. "What did we decide about the Q3 budget?" and it finds the right page. That's genuinely useful for teams.

Obsidian doesn't have official AI. But the community has stepped up. The **Copilot** plugin gives you a chat interface that uses RAG to search your vault while talking to an LLM. **Smart Connections** automatically suggests related notes based on semantic similarity. These work surprisingly well, but you need to configure them yourself — API keys, model selection, indexing settings. It's a weekend project, not a one-click setup.

## What Notion Does Better

**Pros:**
- Team collaboration is unmatched. Real-time editing, comments, mentions, guest access — it just works
- Databases are incredibly powerful. Project tracking, CRM, content calendars, sprint boards — all in one tool
- AI Q&A across your entire workspace is a genuine productivity boost
- Non-technical users can be productive within minutes

**Cons:**
- Your data lives on Notion's servers. Export exists but it's imperfect — you're somewhat locked in
- The markdown "support" is misleading. It's a proprietary format under the hood
- Gets noticeably slow with large workspaces
- $20/month with AI adds up to $240/year for a note-taking app

## What Obsidian Does Better

**Pros:**
- Everything is a plain .md file on your computer. Zero vendor lock-in. If Obsidian disappeared tomorrow, your notes are still there
- The graph view for visualizing connections between notes is genuinely useful for building a knowledge base
- Plugin ecosystem is massive and high-quality. Canvas, Excalidraw, Dataview, Templater — the customization is endless
- Blazing fast, even with thousands of notes. Works fully offline

**Cons:**
- No real collaboration features. This is a solo tool
- Setup takes time. Choosing and configuring plugins is a rabbit hole
- Structured data management (databases, kanban) exists through plugins but isn't as polished as Notion
- Steeper learning curve, especially for non-developers

## The Bottom Line

**Pick Notion if:**
- You work with a team and need shared documents, project tracking, and wikis
- You want databases, kanban boards, and structured views out of the box
- You value "it just works" over customization
- AI-powered workspace search matters to you

**Pick Obsidian if:**
- You're building a personal knowledge base and want to own your data forever
- Privacy and local-first storage are non-negotiable
- You enjoy customizing your tools and don't mind a setup phase
- You want to avoid being locked into any single service

My personal setup? Team projects in Notion, personal notes and research in Obsidian. If I had to pick just one: solo users should go Obsidian, teams should go Notion. I'm pretty confident about that call.
