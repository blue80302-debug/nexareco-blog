Ultralight wireless mice have reached the point where nobody argues about whether they're fast enough. The real questions are whether you need to spend $160, and what the extra money actually buys. This list ranks three of the most-discussed options by what each does best, then tells you who should buy which.

One note on prices before we start. Street prices for all three move constantly. The figures below come from review pages and retailer listings checked while researching this piece, so treat them as a snapshot. Newer siblings such as the Viper V4 Pro and Superlight 2c now exist too, and they are a reason to check current deals.

## At a glance

| | Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2 | Razer Viper V3 Pro | Pulsar X2H |

## 1. Razer Viper V3 Pro: the spec-sheet monster that actually delivers

**Best for: players who want the fastest, lightest, most future-proof flagship.**

Reviewers agree on this one. TweakTown called it 
the lightest mouse it had ever tested
, at 54 g without cable or dongle. PC Gamer pointed out that Razer trimmed about 4 g off the V2 Pro and called it the ultimate esports mouse. ProSettings gave it 5 out of 5. Its conclusion was that the V3 Pro is the new benchmark for gaming mice, because the clicks, build and sensor all hold up in practice and not just on paper.

**Strengths**
- **Polling headroom.** It supports up to 8,000 Hz, and the HyperPolling dongle is in the box. Competitors often charge extra for theirs. PC Gamer lists battery life at 95 hours at 1,000 Hz, dropping to 17 hours at 8,000 Hz. That is the trade-off for anyone chasing the highest polling rate.
- **Build quality.** ProSettings found no rattle when tapping or shaking it, and the shell resisted flex under pressure. RTINGS' head-to-head concluded that the Razer is lighter than the Superlight 2 and has slightly better build quality and feet.
- **Optical switches** and a very accurate optical sensor, which is the main reason it keeps topping "best of" lists.

**Weaknesses**
- **Price.** $159.99 is the headline cost. PC Gamer noted it's not obvious where the extra money went compared with the V2 Pro. Discounting helps: HowToGeek and TweakTown both showed it around $100–110 on Amazon.
- **No Bluetooth.** TweakTown notes you need the proprietary dongle or a cable, so it won't pair with a laptop on the go.
- **Fit.** The shell is small and extremely light. One PC Gamer reviewer found it too small and too light for their taste. ProSettings also raised the idea that there is such a thing as "too light".

**Profile:** You play fast tactical shooters, you have a high-refresh monitor, and you want a mouse that won't be the bottleneck for years. You like small-to-medium symmetrical shapes and want the 8K dongle without paying extra.

## 2. Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2: the safe, proven choice

**Best for: players who value consistency, ecosystem and battery life over spec-sheet bragging rights.**

The Superlight 2 is no longer the lightest or the highest-polling mouse here, but it remains the benchmark for "it just works". PCWorld's review praised its 
quick, accurate 32,000 DPI sensor
 and pointed out that the shape is the same as the original Superlight's, only 3 g lighter at 60 g. It is the heaviest of the three, but 60 g is still very light by any normal standard.

**Strengths**
- **Familiar, widely loved shape.** It's the same footprint as the original (about 4.92 × 2.5 × 1.57 in). If the first Superlight fit your hand, this one will too.
- **Hybrid LIGHTSPEED switches.** A comparison by Tech-Insider credits Logitech with the most mature switch technology of the trio.
- **Sensor specs.** Versus.com lists 888 IPS maximum speed and 88 G acceleration, against 650 IPS and 50 G for the Pulsar X2H eS. It also lists a 100 DPI minimum.
- **Battery and software.** It's rated at up to roughly 95 hours at default settings, and G Hub supports onboard profiles. The Pulsar's 70-hour figure is a third-party estimate.
- **Price flexibility.** PCWorld's embedded retailer data showed about $153 at the time of its review, and Versus listed an Amazon price near $114. The list price is high, but sales are frequent.

**Weaknesses**
- **Weight.** It's 6 g heavier than the Viper and 6–8 g heavier than the Pulsar. That sounds trivial but matters if you're chasing the absolute lightest setup.
- **Polling.** It tops out at 2,000 Hz natively. Tech-Insider flags this as the limiting factor against the Razer's 8K, though plenty of players will never notice it.
- **Value.** At full price it costs the same as the Razer while doing less on paper.

**Profile:** You've used a Superlight before, you want zero fuss, and you'd rather use G Hub than learn a new ecosystem. You also care about battery life and want a mouse that has been tried by many pros and players for a long time.

## 3. Pulsar X2H: the value pick with real credibility

**Best for: budget-conscious players, claw grippers and anyone who wants flagship weight without the flagship price.**

TechPowerUp lists the X2H at $99.95. That is about $60 less than the other two at list price, a gap Tech-Insider also highlighted. The regular size weighs 54 g and the Mini 52 g, which TechPowerUp's own scale confirmed. It keeps the PixArt PAW3395 sensor and optical main switches. TechPowerUp gave it a Recommended award, with a caveat: 
assuming one gets a good copy
.

**Strengths**
- **Shape variety.** The X2H differs from the X2V2 mainly in having a high hump, and TechPowerUp notes it faces less competition in that shape category. It comes in two sizes, so smaller hands are covered.
- **Weight and price combination.** Compared with the Razer Cobra Pro at 77 g and $129.99, TechPowerUp found the Pulsar far lighter and cheaper. It also beats the $159.99 G-Wolves Hati-S Plus 4K on value.
- **4K polling is affordable.** The 4K dongle costs $19.95. That's an extra expense, but the total is still well below either flagship.
- **Upgrade path.** If you want to go lighter, EloShapes reviewed the X2H CrazyLight Mini at 39 g and $129.95, with a custom XS-1 sensor (based on the PAW3950) and 8K support.

**Weaknesses**
- **Quality control and click feel.** TechPowerUp's "good copy" caveat and its note about a slightly odd click feel are the main flags. Pre-travel isn't to everyone's taste.
- **Ecosystem polish.** Versus.com's scoring puts the X2H V3 at 60 points against 82 for the Superlight 2 and 76 for the Viper V3 Pro. Its spec table also lists no onboard profile memory on the X2H eS and a 500 DPI minimum.
- **Battery.** About 70 hours is an estimate from third parties, below the other two.
- **Naming confusion.** Pulsar has X2H, X2H eS, X2H V3 and CrazyLight variants, and prices vary. A review at GearScout listed it at $79, against TechPowerUp's $99.95 MSRP. Check which exact variant you're buying.

**Profile:** You want to get into ultralight wireless without spending $160. You're a claw or fingertip gripper, and you're comfortable checking your unit for defects when it arrives.

## So, which one should you buy?

- **Pick the Razer Viper V3 Pro** if you want the best all-round hardware and plan to use high polling rates. It's also the clear winner if you can find it near $100–110.
- **Pick the Logitech Superlight 2** if you like the proven shape and the software. It suits players who want reliability with long battery life, and it makes the most sense on sale.
- **Pick the Pulsar X2H** if budget matters. You get a similar weight at roughly 60% of the price, as long as you accept the trade-offs in polish and the risk of a bad unit.

**Our overall pick:** the Viper V3 Pro wins on raw capability, and the X2H wins on value per dollar. The Superlight 2 sits in between as the low-risk option. If you can't decide, buy whichever is on sale. At these weights and sensor specs, the gap in your performance will come down to which shape fits your hand.
