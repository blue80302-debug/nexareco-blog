I keep getting the same DM: "Should I learn Figma, Sketch or Canva?" My answer used to be "it depends," which helps nobody. So I spent a few weeks doing the same set of jobs in all three. I built a landing page mockup, a small design system with buttons, cards and form states, a pitch deck, and a batch of social posts. This is what I found.

**Quick note on the numbers:** the prices and limits below come from each vendor's public pricing page as I last saw them. All three companies change plans often, especially Figma with its seat-type model. Check the official pages before you pay for anything.

## The short version

- **Figma** is the one I'd pick for most product and UI work. It's browser-based, and real-time collaboration is its best feature.
- **Sketch** is still a polished, fast Mac-native tool. It only makes sense if your whole team is on Macs.
- **Canva** isn't really a UI tool. It's the best of the three for marketing visuals, decks and social posts, especially if nobody on your team is a designer.

## Figma: the one I open every morning

**What I liked**

1. **Zero friction to share.** I sent a link and a client was commenting on my frames within 30 seconds. They didn't install anything or create an account to look.
2. **Components and variants.** Building a button with size, state and icon variants took me about 15 minutes. Variables for colors, spacing and light/dark modes took the system from "pretty" to "actually maintainable."
3. **Prototyping is in the same file.** I didn't need to export and re-import to test a flow.
4. **The ecosystem.** Whatever I needed, whether icon packs, a UI kit or a plugin to generate realistic data, someone had already built it.

**What annoyed me**

- **The seat pricing is confusing.** Full seats, Dev seats and viewer seats cost different amounts, and a small team can overspend without noticing.
- **It's a browser tool at heart.** On huge files with hundreds of frames, I saw lag that I didn't see in Sketch.
- **The free tier is a trial in practice.** It's fine for learning, but you'll hit the file limits quickly if you work with clients.

## Sketch: still great, but narrower than it used to be

I started my career in Sketch, so I went in biased. It's still beautiful to use. The native Mac app feels snappy, the vector tools are precise, and the interface is clean.

**What I liked**

- **Speed on a Mac.** Big artboards pan and zoom smoothly, and the app feels more "native" than Figma.
- **Pricing is simple.** It's a flat per-editor price, and viewers are free.
- **Vector and icon work.** I still find the pen and boolean operations pleasant.

**What annoyed me**

- **Mac only for editing.** If one contractor uses Windows, they can view files in the browser but can't really work in them. That's a dealbreaker for many teams.
- **Collaboration feels bolted on.** It works, but it isn't the "we're all in the file" experience Figma gives you.
- **The ecosystem is smaller.** Most of the community's new UI kits and templates are Figma-first now.

## Canva: not a rival, but I use it more than I expected

I opened Canva expecting to dismiss it as "not a pro tool." Then I made a full pitch deck and eight social posts in under an hour, and Figma would have taken me three times as long.

**What I liked**

- **Templates.** Thousands of them, and many look good with little editing.
- **Brand Kit.** Fonts, colors and logos are one click away, so non-designers stay on-brand.
- **Price versus value.** Pro is roughly the cost of a couple of coffees a month, and it bundles stock assets, background removal, resizing and AI features.
- **Anyone can use it.** I handed it to a coworker with no design background, and she was productive in ten minutes.

**What annoyed me**

- **It isn't built for UI/UX.** There's no real component logic, auto layout or proper developer handoff.
- **It's easy to make something that looks like everyone else's.** Templates are a double-edged sword.
- **Precision is limited.** Fine typographic and vector control is behind Figma and Sketch.

## Who should pick what

**Pick Figma if:**
- You design apps or websites, or work on a product team.
- You work with developers and need real-time collaboration.
- You're a student or aspiring UI/UX designer. Most job listings and portfolio examples assume Figma.

**Pick Sketch if:**
- Your whole team is on Macs, and you have an existing Sketch library you don't want to migrate.
- You prefer a native, offline-friendly app and a simple per-editor price.

**Pick Canva if:**
- You make presentations, social posts, flyers or YouTube thumbnails.
- You're a founder, marketer or small business owner without a designer.
- You need speed and consistency more than pixel-level control.

## My verdict

If I could keep only one, it'd be **Figma**. It covers the widest range of professional work, and the collaboration is hard to beat. I'd still keep **Canva** open next to it, because for decks and social content it's faster than anything else I've tried, and the free plan costs nothing to try. I'd only recommend **Sketch** to a Mac-only team that already uses it and likes it.

If you're starting from zero, learn Figma for the skills and use Canva for quick marketing work. Both have free plans, so you can test them for a week without spending anything.
