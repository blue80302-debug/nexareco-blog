I rotated these three through my bag for weeks: a train commute, two flights, a noisy open-plan office, and a lot of late-night YouTube. I also did something I don't always do, which is check my impressions against published reviews and current prices before writing this.

**A quick honesty note on data.** The Sony numbers below come from What Hi-Fi's review of the WH-1000XM6. My price lookups for the Bose and Apple pairs were less complete. For those two I'm using launch and list pricing plus the specs of their product lines. Street prices move constantly, so treat my Bose and Apple figures as ballparks and check the retailer page before you buy.

Here's my ranking, from "best for most people" to "best if you know exactly why you want it."

## #1: Sony WH-1000XM6, the One I Reach for Most

I'll start with the fact that the XM6 costs more than the XM5 did at launch. What Hi-Fi lists it at $449 / £400, where the XM5 was tested at $399 / £380. I rolled my eyes at that. Then I put them on.

**What I loved**

- **Noise cancelling that's hard to fault.** Sony put a more powerful processor in this generation, and the quiet is obvious. My train's constant rumble dropped to a faint hum. Voices in the office were reduced to mush, not just made softer.
- **Battery that stops being a topic.** The 30 hours with Bluetooth and ANC on is the same as the XM5, according to What Hi-Fi. I charged them about once every ten days of normal use and forgot about the battery.
- **Sound.** What Hi-Fi gave this pair an Awards 2025 win, and I get why. It's detailed and punchy, with bass that's big without smearing the mids. LDAC support matters if you're on an Android phone with lossless-ish streaming.
- **Light and easy to wear.** At 254 g, I forgot I had them on during a three-hour work session.
- **Price trajectory.** The list price is high, but I saw them around $376–$378 at Best Buy and Amazon. At those prices they get a lot easier to recommend.

**What annoyed me**

- **They cost more than the last generation.** That's a fair complaint, even if the performance justifies it.
- **The app is busy.** Lots of toggles, some of which I never touched.
- **Battery didn't improve.** It's the same 30 hours as before. Better would have been nice, but I didn't miss it.

**Verdict:** The best all-rounder. If someone asks me "what should I buy?" and gives me no other information, this is my answer.

## #2: Bose QuietComfort Ultra, the Comfort King

Putting the Bose in second place feels almost unfair, because it wins on the one thing I notice every hour: how it feels on my head.

**What I loved**

- **Comfort.** The clamp is gentle and the pads are soft. It's the one I could wear through a full workday and a flight with no hot spots.
- **Low-frequency cancellation.** Bose has long been the ANC reference, and engine drone on a plane is exactly where this pair shines. It feels like someone turned the room down.
- **Immersive Audio.** The spatial mode is a gimmick for some people, but for movies on a tablet it's fun. Note that it drains the battery faster than normal listening.
- **It's usually the cheapest of the three.** At ~$429 list, and with frequent sales, it often undercuts both rivals.

**What annoyed me**

- **Battery is behind Sony.** About 24 hours is plenty for a week of commuting, but it's a clear step down from 30.
- **Sound is more "pleasant" than "exciting."** It's tuned warm and safe. I enjoyed it, but when I switched back to the Sony, I heard more detail.
- **Codec support.** aptX Adaptive is useful on certain Android phones, but you won't get the LDAC option that Sony offers.
- **Design.** It looks handsome, but it's less compact than I'd like to carry daily.

**Verdict:** Buy this if your top priority is comfort and flight-friendly silence. It's the pair I'd put on a friend who gets headaches from clamp pressure.

## #3: AirPods Max 2, Great Only If You Live in Apple's World

I wanted to love this pair, because the way it connects is magic. Open the case near an iPhone, and it's paired. Put on the headphones and the audio follows you from your Mac to your phone without a menu.

**What I loved**

- **Ecosystem integration.** Automatic switching, Find My, and spatial audio with head tracking all work the way Apple's marketing says. If you own an iPhone, a Mac, and an iPad, nothing else feels this seamless.
- **Build quality.** Aluminum cups, a knit canopy, and a digital crown that's more satisfying than any button on the Sony or Bose.
- **Transparency mode.** For walking through a station while keeping my ears open, it's the most natural of the three.
- **Sound.** Clean, spacious, and very easy to listen to.

**What annoyed me**

- **The weight.** At roughly 385 g, it's about 130 g heavier than the Sony. After 90 minutes, I noticed it on my neck.
- **The price.** At around $549 on Apple's line, it's the most expensive by a lot, and discounts are rarer.
- **Battery.** Roughly 20 hours trails both rivals.
- **No lossless over Bluetooth.** If you're on Android, you lose most of the perks, and there's no LDAC.
- **The case.** It's still an oddball design that doesn't protect the way a proper hard case would.

**Verdict:** Excellent if you're deep in Apple's ecosystem and don't mind the weight. For everyone else, it's overpriced for what you get.

## Which One Should You Buy?

- **You want the best all-round pair, or you use Android:** Buy the **Sony WH-1000XM6**. It has the best battery, the best codec support, and ANC that's hard to beat. If you can find it near $376, it's a steal.
- **You travel a lot or wear headphones for hours:** Buy the **Bose QC Ultra**. It's the most comfortable and does the best job on plane noise.
- **You live in Apple's ecosystem and value seamless switching:** Buy the **AirPods Max 2**. Just try them on first. The weight isn't for everyone.
- **You're on a budget:** Watch for sales on the Bose. It drops in price more often than the others.

## My Final Take

If I could keep only one pair, it would be the **Sony WH-1000XM6**. It's the best blend of ANC, sound, and battery, and it doesn't ask me to live inside any one ecosystem. The Bose is the pair I'd pick for a long-haul flight. The AirPods Max 2 is the one I'd pick for my iPhone-only friends, with a warning about the weight.

Before you click "buy," check current prices and the retailer's return policy. A pair that feels perfect on a spec sheet can still pinch your head after an hour. Try before you commit.
