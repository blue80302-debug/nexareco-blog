I'll admit I came into this comparison with a bias. I've lived in macOS for years, and every time Dell announces a "MacBook killer," I roll my eyes a little. But the 2026 XPS 16 got under my skin. It's the first XPS in a while that I wanted to like, so I spent a few weeks switching between it and the MacBook Pro M4 for writing, photo edits, video exports, and a lot of coffee shop work.

The short version is that both laptops are very good, and they're good at different things. One of them is also a lot easier to recommend without caveats.

## The Spec Sheet, Side by Side

Before the feelings, here are the numbers. Dell's figures come from the Tom's Guide, Tom's Hardware, and PCWorld reviews of the 2026 XPS 16. The MacBook figures are Apple's published specs for the M4 generation. Apple's prices move around with retailer discounts now that the M4 is a generation old, so check current deals.

| | MacBook Pro 14" (M4) | Dell XPS 16 (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting price** | ~$1,599 (16GB / 512GB) | $1,749 (Core Ultra 5 325, 16GB / 512GB) |
| **Price as tested/typical high config** | 16" M4 Pro models start around $2,499 | $2,349 (Core Ultra X7 358H, 32GB, 1TB OLED) |
| **Chip** | Apple M4 (10-core CPU) | Intel Core Ultra X7 358H (Panther Lake) |
| **GPU** | 10-core Apple GPU | Intel Arc B390 (integrated) |
| **RAM** | 16GB unified (up to 32GB) | 16–64GB LPDDR5x (32GB at 9600 MT/s on the tested unit) |
| **Display** | 14.2" Liquid Retina XDR, 120Hz | 16" 3200×2000 tandem OLED touch, 20–120Hz |
| **Ports** | 3× Thunderbolt 4, HDMI, SD card, MagSafe, headphone jack | 3× Thunderbolt 4/USB-C, headphone jack |
| **Weight** | ~3.4 lb (14") | ~3.65 lb (1.66 kg) |
| **OS** | macOS | Windows 11 |

Notice that the two don't line up perfectly. The XPS 16 is a 16-inch machine, and the 14-inch MacBook Pro is the one that matches it on price. If you want a screen the same size as the Dell's, the 16-inch MacBook Pro costs noticeably more.

## Price: Closer Than You'd Think, Until You Spec Them Up

The XPS 16 starts at $1,749. 
Tom's Hardware tested the flagship configuration at $2,349 with the Core Ultra X7 358H, 32GB of RAM, and a 1TB SSD.
 That's the version with the OLED touchscreen, and it's the one I'd actually buy. The $1,749 base model uses a 1920×1200 non-touch panel, which feels like the wrong place to save money on a laptop this size.

The MacBook Pro M4 is the cheaper way into a premium laptop. The catch is that Apple's RAM and storage upgrades are famously painful. A 14-inch with 32GB and 1TB gets uncomfortably close to the Dell's price. Even so, 16GB of unified memory on an M-series chip goes a long way. Windows laptops usually need more RAM to feel as smooth.

## Screen: Dell Wins on Wow, Apple Wins on Consistency

The XPS 16's tandem OLED is gorgeous. 
Tom's Guide's review praises the slick design, the Panther Lake performance, the long battery life, and the tandem OLED panel.
 I agree about the panel. Black levels and color pop in a way that makes the MacBook's mini-LED look merely excellent. Watching HDR video on it was a treat, and it's also a touchscreen if you like that.

The MacBook's Liquid Retina XDR is still one of the best laptop displays you can buy. It's bright, calibrated out of the box, and gets very good outdoors. It doesn't have the OLED's perfect blacks, but it has no burn-in worries and the color accuracy is spot on for photo work. The 14-inch size is less immersive than the Dell's 16-inch canvas, though. When I was editing timelines, I missed the extra space every time I went back to the Mac.

## Performance and Battery: Where Intel Is Back in the Fight, Mostly

This is the most interesting part. Intel's Panther Lake chips are much better than the old Meteor Lake and Lunar Lake generations. 
Tom's Guide calls the XPS 16 an excellent showcase for Panther Lake.
 The integrated Arc graphics handle light creative work and even some gaming. The XPS 16 no longer offers a discrete GPU, which Dell had to give up to get this thinner design. 
TechRadar noted that the redesign drops the discrete graphics option for the foreseeable future.


The M4 is still an efficiency monster. In my daily use, the MacBook stayed cool and silent through things that made the Dell's fans audible, like batch photo exports and a 4K video render. For sustained heavy loads on battery, the Apple chip is hard to beat. PCWorld's review listed "lags behind in performance" among the XPS 16's cons, and that matches my sense that Dell is competitive but not ahead.

Battery life is where the two reviewers I read disagreed. Tom's Guide praised the XPS 16's endurance, while XDA Developers said 
battery life could be better
. In my own use, the MacBook got through a full workday with plenty left over, and I wasn't thinking about where the charger was. With the XPS 16 I was more aware of the battery percentage, especially with the OLED at high brightness.

## Design, Keyboard, and Ports: Dell's Weak Spots

The XPS 16 looks stunning, with a flat, minimal, futuristic look. But it has real usability trade-offs. The ports are three Thunderbolt 4/USB-C and a headphone jack, and that's it. The MacBook Pro gives you HDMI, an SD card slot, and MagSafe. If you're a photographer or you present on external screens, you won't need a dongle bag with the Mac.

The keyboard and the haptic touchpad are also divisive. 
PCWorld's negatives list for the XPS 16 includes few ports, a high price, and a keyboard it calls "still problematic."
 XDA also mentioned that the keyboard takes some getting used to, and that there's no fingerprint reader. I got used to the keys after a few days, but I never loved them the way I love the MacBook's keyboard. Apple's trackpad is still the best on the market.

## Pros and Cons

**MacBook Pro M4**
- ✅ Excellent efficiency: cool, quiet, and long battery life
- ✅ Best-in-class trackpad, speakers, and build quality
- ✅ Great port selection (HDMI, SD, MagSafe)
- ✅ Strong resale value
- ❌ Expensive RAM and storage upgrades
- ❌ Only 14 inches at this price point
- ❌ macOS limits gaming and some Windows-only software

**Dell XPS 16 (2026)**
- ✅ Beautiful 16-inch tandem OLED touchscreen
- ✅ Sleek design and strong Panther Lake performance
- ✅ Upgradeable up to 64GB RAM
- ✅ Runs Windows and everything that needs it
- ❌ Only three USB-C ports
- ❌ Keyboard takes getting used to, and there's no fingerprint reader
- ❌ Battery life is inconsistent
- ❌ Pricey for what you get

## So Which One Should You Buy?

**Buy the MacBook Pro M4 if:**
- You value battery life and quiet operation above everything else.
- You edit photos or video, code, or write all day.
- You want the best keyboard, trackpad, and speakers.
- You're already in the Apple ecosystem.
- You want the best value for the money.

**Buy the Dell XPS 16 if:**
- You need Windows for work software or you want to play games on the side.
- You want a bigger, more beautiful OLED screen and a touchscreen.
- You want to upgrade to 64GB of RAM without paying Apple's markup.
- You can live with a few ports and a quirky keyboard.

## My Verdict

I expected to write that the Dell had finally caught up. In some ways it has: the screen is better, the design is sharper, and Panther Lake is a real step forward. But for most people, the MacBook Pro M4 is still the safer buy. It's more efficient, it has better ports, and it's more pleasant to use all day. It also costs less at the entry level.

The XPS 16 is the one I'd recommend to Windows people who want something special, not to Mac users thinking about switching. If you're on the fence, go to a store and type on both. Your hands will tell you more than any spec sheet.
