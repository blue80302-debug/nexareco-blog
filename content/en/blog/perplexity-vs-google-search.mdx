I've been a Google person for more than twenty years. My fingers type "g" and Chrome autocompletes before I decide anything. A while back I started opening Perplexity in a second tab for research-style questions. After a few weeks of using both side by side for work, shopping, and troubleshooting, I'm now clear on which one I reach for in each situation.

> **Data note:** My live web search tool failed while I was writing this. The prices and figures below come from my own knowledge of public sources. Those are Perplexity's and Google's pricing pages, company statements, and the Tow Center (Columbia Journalism Review) study of AI search citations. They may be out of date by October 2026, so check the current pricing pages before you pay for anything.

## The short version

- **Perplexity** works like a research assistant. You ask a question and it returns a written answer with numbered sources.
- **Google Search** is still the best index of the web. It has also become partly an AI product, with AI Overviews at the top of results and AI Mode as a chat-style tab.

The old picture of Perplexity as the AI one and Google as the link list no longer holds. Google now does a lot of what Perplexity does, and that makes the choice harder.

## Price: both are free until you want the extras

Perplexity's free tier is useful. You get unlimited basic searches and a small daily allowance of Pro searches. Pro is about $20/month, and it unlocks more Pro searches, model selection, file uploads, and heavier Deep Research use. The Max tier at about $200/month is for people who live in the tool, and I think it's overkill for almost everyone.

Google Search costs nothing, and that's hard to beat. Google now bundles AI features into its Google AI Pro plan at roughly the same $20/month, along with Gemini, storage, and Workspace features. Plain Google search, Maps, and Lens don't require a subscription.

**Verdict on price:** Google wins for casual users. At $20/month the two are comparable, but Perplexity's paid plan is more focused on search.

## What I liked about Perplexity

1. **It saves me clicks.** I asked "What's the difference between Postgres logical and physical replication, and when would I use each?" I got a clean answer in about ten seconds, with six sources. On Google I would have opened four tabs and skimmed three of them.
2. **Follow-ups feel natural.** I can say "now explain that for a small team with no DBA" and it keeps the thread. Google's classic search forgets everything between queries.
3. **Citations are always visible.** I click a number and I'm at the source. It encourages checking in a way that a block of unsourced text doesn't.
4. **The interface is calm.** No sponsored blocks in the way, no SEO listicles, no "10 best X" pages written for crawlers.
5. **Model choice.** On Pro I can switch between models, which is handy when one handles a particular question better.

## What annoyed me about Perplexity

1. **Accuracy still isn't guaranteed.** The Tow Center study in 2025 tested AI search tools on attributing news excerpts. All of them were wrong a lot, and Perplexity did best of the group and still got roughly a third of queries wrong. Citations make errors easier to catch, but they don't prevent them.
2. **It sometimes cites weak sources.** Once or twice I saw an answer lean on a Reddit thread or a low-quality blog and present it with confidence.
3. **Local and shopping queries are weak.** "Good ramen near me open now" belongs in Google Maps. Perplexity is only okay at it.
4. **Navigational searches are slower.** If I just want my bank's login page, going through an answer engine is pointless.

## What I liked about Google Search

1. **Nothing matches its breadth.** Obscure forum posts, official docs, maps, flights, images, and video all work from one box.
2. **Speed for simple queries.** Weather, unit conversions, sports scores, and "what time does X close" all come back instantly.
3. **AI Overviews and AI Mode are improving.** Google says AI Overviews reach well over a billion users a month. AI Mode handles multi-part questions and follow-ups, which was Perplexity's main advantage.
4. **Local, maps, shopping, and travel integration** is where Google is simply far ahead.

## What annoyed me about Google Search

1. **Ads and clutter.** On commercial queries, I scroll past sponsored results, AI boxes, "People also ask" panels, and videos before I reach an organic link.
2. **AI Overviews can be wrong.** They're prominent, so a bad one is more visible, and I've seen confident answers that were slightly off.
3. **SEO sludge.** For product research I often hit affiliate pages that say nothing useful. This is the main reason I started using Perplexity.
4. **Weaker context between queries.** In classic search I have to re-type everything.

## Head-to-head by use case

## My workflow now

I begin with Perplexity for anything that starts with "explain", "compare", or "what's the best way to". I use Google for anything that starts with "where", "when", or a site name. When Perplexity gives me an important claim, I click through to the source. That takes ten seconds and has caught a few mistakes.

## Who should choose what

**Choose Perplexity if you:**
- research for a living (students, analysts, writers, developers);
- hate wading through SEO junk and ads;
- ask long, multi-part questions and want a sourced summary;
- would pay $20/month for a research tool.

**Choose Google Search if you:**
- mostly search for local places, directions, shopping, news, or quick facts;
- want free with no limits;
- already use Gmail, Maps, and Android, and value the integration;
- want to see many sources and make your own judgment.

**Use both if you:**
- do serious research and everyday errands. This is me. Google is free, so you lose nothing by keeping it, and Perplexity's free tier is enough to find out whether you like it.

## Final verdict

Perplexity isn't a Google killer. It's a better tool for one kind of search: understanding something quickly, with sources. For that job it beats Google's classic results, and for me it's often faster than Google's own AI Mode. For everything else, including maps, local, news, shopping, and navigation, Google is still the default and I don't see that changing soon.

If I had to pick only one, I'd keep Google, because it covers more of my daily searches and costs nothing. I would still miss Perplexity every time I sat down to research something properly. Try the free tier for a week, and upgrade to Pro only if you find yourself hitting the daily limit.
