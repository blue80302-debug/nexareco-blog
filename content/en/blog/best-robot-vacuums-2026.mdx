Robot vacuum shopping in 2026 is a mess. Every box says "AI," every spec sheet shows a huge Pascal number, and the list prices are fantasy. I got tired of it and decided to rank the three machines that keep coming up in my DMs.

**A note on how I did this.** This is a research-driven ranking. I built it from lab-style reviews, spec sheets, and owner feedback, not from a three-way floor test in my apartment. The search tool I used also cut out before I could pull fresh listings for every model. The Roborock numbers below are well sourced. For the Dreame and the Roomba, treat the prices and some specs as ballparks and check the live listing before you buy.

## The quick ranking

1. **Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra**: best all-rounder if you catch it on sale
2. **Dreame X40 Ultra**: best raw power and value
3. **iRobot Roomba j9+**: best for simple, vacuum-only households

## 1. Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra: the one I'd pick for most people

This is the most thoroughly documented of the three, so I'll start here.

**Price reality check.** TechRadar lists it at $1,799.99, and Alex Reviews Tech notes AU$2,999 in Australia. Nobody should pay that. Roborock's own listing on TechRadar's price widget showed $1,099.99, down from $1,899.99. RoboVacGuide says the "now-common" sale price is $899–$999. At full MSRP, that same guide calls it a terrible buy, and I agree. At $899–$1,099 it's very good. Robotomated also reports Roborock cut its price from $1,799 to $1,599 in 2025 because of competition from the Dreame X40.

**What I like:**
- **The dock.** An 8-in-1 dock that handles emptying, hot-water mop washing (around 140°F), heated-air drying, water refill, and detergent. One review put hands-off time at 4–7 weeks. A bag lasting about seven weeks is what makes a robot feel automatic.
- **Carpet handling.** The mops lift 20mm, so a robot that mops your kitchen won't soak your rug. Alex Reviews Tech specifically called out that this kept carpets from getting damp.
- **Software.** Matter, Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and Siri Shortcuts are all supported.
- **Running costs.** Modern Castle estimated yearly upkeep at about **$32**, basically dock bags.

**What bugs me:**
- Modern Castle said it **struggled with long hair tangles**. If you have long-haired people or pets, that matters.
- The 270 ml dustbin and small water tanks mean more refilling than the specs suggest. Modern Castle flagged this too.
- It's **2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only**, per Everyday Home Comfort. This is annoying if your router is picky.
- The 10,000 Pa figure is no longer special. TechRadar pointed out that Ecovacs' T30S Combo claims 11,000 Pa.
- The dock is big: about 16 × 16.5 × 18.5 inches.

## 2. Dreame X40 Ultra: the power-per-dollar pick

Several reviewers call the Dreame the S8 MaxV Ultra's most direct rival. One guide describes it as offering higher peak suction at a slightly lower price.

**Pros:**
- Higher claimed suction (12,000 Pa vs 10,000 Pa).
- Hot-water mop washing and drying in the dock, same class as the Roborock.
- Its extendable mop and side brush are meant to help in corners and along edges.
- It's often cheaper than the Roborock, and it pushed Roborock into cutting prices.

**Cons:**
- Roborock's app and software are more mature. The Roborock guides I read kept citing its track record and its Smart Plan feature.
- Suction numbers are marketing. TechRadar's point applies here: comparing Pa across brands is hard.
- I'd double-check long-term reliability and parts availability before paying full price.

**My take:** if the Dreame is $100+ cheaper than the Roborock on the day you shop, buy it. If the prices are close, I'd go Roborock for the software.

## 3. iRobot Roomba j9+: the "I just want it to vacuum" option

The j9+ isn't competing on mopping. It's a vacuum with a Clean Base auto-empty dock, and its selling point is obstacle avoidance. iRobot has long marketed its camera-based PrecisionVision, and it backs this with a pet-waste promise (P.O.O.P.).

**Pros:**
- Simple. There are no mop pads to wash or dock water tanks to refill.
- Good fit for homes with lots of carpet and rugs.
- Mature app and a large user base.

**Cons:**
- **No mopping.** If you want a robot that mops, you need the pricier Combo version or a different model.
- Lower suction on paper and a smaller feature set than the other two.
- **Company uncertainty.** iRobot's financial troubles and ownership change late last year are worth a quick check on support and warranty before you buy.

## Verdict: who should buy what

- **Buy the Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra** if you want the most proven, most hands-off setup and can get it near $900–$1,100. It's best for mixed carpet and hard floors, and for people who hate maintenance.
- **Buy the Dreame X40 Ultra** if you want maximum suction for the money and don't mind a slightly younger software platform. It's also a good option if you find a deal that undercuts the Roborock.
- **Buy the Roomba j9+** if you only want vacuuming, have lots of carpet, and prefer simplicity over a feature-packed dock.
- **Skip all three** if your budget is under $500. RoboVacGuide argues newer mid-range robots match 10,000 Pa suction for half the price, and I think that's true.

If you're torn between the first two, **wait for a sale and buy whichever is cheaper that week.** They're close enough that the price should decide it.
